# Code of conduct

Lexr is a small project, but everyone who takes part should be able to do so
without being belittled, harassed, or made to feel unsafe.

## Our promise

We welcome people of every background and level of experience. Participation
should be free from harassment and discrimination based on identity, ability,
appearance, health, nationality, beliefs, or any other personal characteristic.

You do not have to agree with every decision. You do have to treat people with
care. Review the work, not the person; explain disagreements without turning
them into personal attacks; and leave room for someone to learn or change their
mind.

## What does not belong here

The following conduct is not acceptable:

- discriminatory language, slurs, or degrading jokes;
- harassment, threats, intimidation, or unwanted sexual attention;
- sharing someone else's private information without clear permission;
- publishing private device data, credentials, or identifying diagnostics;
- sustained disruption, personal attacks, or deliberately hostile replies; and
- retaliation against someone who raises a concern in good faith.

## Where this applies

This code applies in the Lexr repository, issues, pull requests, reviews, and
other project spaces. It also applies when someone is speaking on behalf of
Lexr or presenting themselves as a project representative.

## Reporting a problem

Please report conduct concerns privately to Leon Silcott at
[leon@boasi.io](mailto:leon@boasi.io). Do not put sensitive details in a public
issue. Share only what is needed to understand what happened, along with links
or context that may help.

I will keep a report as private as I reasonably can, consider the context, and
tell the reporter what action I take when it is safe and appropriate to do so.
This is a single-maintainer project, so there is not yet an independent
in-project escalation path. If a report concerns me, or reporting to me does
not feel safe, please use the safety or abuse-reporting route provided by the
platform where the conduct happened. That limitation is real, and I would
rather state it plainly than promise a process the project does not have.

## Enforcement

The response will depend on the context, severity, and whether the conduct is
part of a pattern. It may include a private conversation, removal of content, a
warning, a temporary restriction, or a permanent ban from project spaces.
Immediate steps may be taken first when they are needed to protect someone's
safety or privacy.

The goal is a project where people can do thoughtful work together. Thank you
for helping keep it that way.
